# Access Infrastructure governance

**Status:** organisational design / claim discipline  
**Public claim:** none — council not yet constituted as a legal body in this repository

## Access Infrastructure Council

MapAble should establish a paid **Access Infrastructure Council** with direct lived experience across:

- physical disability;
- blindness / low vision;
- Deaf and hard-of-hearing communities;
- communication disability / AAC;
- intellectual disability;
- neurodivergence;
- psychosocial disability;
- acquired brain injury;
- chronic / episodic disability;
- multiple disability.

Representation must deliberately include First Nations people, CALD communities, regional/remote participants, children/young people and older disabled people — Australian evidence shows assistance and access needs differ across these populations.

## Council remit

Govern, in order:

1. ontology changes and deprecations;
2. evidence rules and freshness policies;
3. user research priorities;
4. accessibility testing standards;
5. discovery scoring (presentation only);
6. algorithm / compatibility evaluation;
7. dispute resolution for contested observations;
8. public claims and marketing language.

Engineers are implementers, not the ultimate arbiters of what constitutes accessibility.

## Claim discipline

Until the council (or an interim documented review) approves promotion:

- no “national accessibility registry” claims;
- no “certified accessible” claims from MapAble scores;
- no substitution of accreditation for personal compatibility;
- Access Infrastructure APIs remain documentation / shadow / flag-gated.

## Change process for ontology

1. Propose concept or domain change with evidence requirements and prohibited inferences.
2. Map standards references (optional) without asserting compliance.
3. Council or interim review.
4. Publish new ontology version; retain aliases.
5. Update compilers, fixtures and tests before enabling flags.
